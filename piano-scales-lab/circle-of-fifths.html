Circle of Fifths

A simple map of how the 12 keys relate to each other

The circle arranges all 12 keys in the order of fifths. The outer ring shows the major keys and the inner ring shows each major key's relative minor (the minor key that shares the same notes). Under each major key is its key signature, the number of sharps or flats.

  • Moving clockwise adds one sharp each step (C, G, D, A, E, B)
  • Moving anticlockwise adds one flat each step (C, F, B♭, E♭, A♭, D♭)
  • At the bottom, F♯/G♭ is the same key written two ways (6 sharps or 6 flats)

Where to find it

  • Scales page — tap ◔ Circle of fifths above the key buttons to open it full screen
  • Info page — a circle also sits under the key signature card

Choosing a key

Tap any key on the circle to select it. Tapping the outer ring picks the major key; tapping the inner ring picks the minor key. If you pick a minor key while on a major scale type (or the other way round), the scale type switches to match — for example Major becomes Natural Minor, and Major Pentatonic becomes Minor Pentatonic. The centre of the circle always shows the selected key, its signature and its relative.

Playing from the circle screen

The full-screen version has the same Relative, Parallel and Key +3 buttons, the right-hand keyboard and the same play bar as the Scales page.

  • Playback here is right hand only, to match the single keyboard on screen
  • While a scale is looping, tapping a key on the circle queues the key change for the end of the loop, exactly like the key buttons on the Scales page
  • Opening the circle screen stops anything that was playing on the Scales page, and closing it stops playback started here

To practise the keys closest to the one you know, play a scale, then tap the key on either side of it on the circle.
