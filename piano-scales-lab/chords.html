Chord Progressions

Exploring chords and progressions in any key

The Chords page shows every chord in the current key on a scrollable piano keyboard, with a library of progressions to loop and practise over.

Chord Type

At the top of the sidebar, six chord type buttons replace the usual scale type selector when you are on the Chords tab.

  • Major — the seven diatonic triads of the major scale (I ii iii IV V vi vii°)
  • Nat Min — the seven diatonic triads of the natural minor scale
  • Maj 7 — seventh chords built on the major scale (Imaj7 iim7 iiim7 IVmaj7 V7 vim7 vii°7)
  • Min 7 — seventh chords built on the natural minor scale
  • Dom 7 — dominant seventh chord on every degree
  • Dim 7 — diminished seventh chord on every degree

Dom 7 and Dim 7 do not show chord progressions — they are reference modes for exploring those chord types across all degrees.

Chord Display

Tap any chord row to highlight its notes on the keyboard and hear it play. The chord name, Roman numeral and note names are shown.

Inversions

Each chord has inversion buttons — Root, 1st and 2nd for triads, Root, 1st, 2nd and 3rd for seventh chords. Tap any to hear the inversion and see it on the keyboard.

Chord Progressions

Scroll down below the chord list to find a library of common progressions — Pop, Blues, Jazz, Classical and more. Available for Major, Nat Min, Maj 7 and Min 7 modes.

  • ▶ plays the progression once through
  • ↻ loops continuously — press again to play out the current bar then stop
  • Tap the BPM number to set your tempo

Key Changes While Looping

While a progression is looping, press Key +3, Relative or Parallel to queue a key change. It fires at the end of the current loop with no interruption.

Relative and Parallel

  • Relative — jumps to the relative major or minor (same notes, different root)
  • Parallel — jumps to the parallel major or minor (same root, different notes)

Relative is great for practising a major key and its relative minor back to back in one smooth loop.
